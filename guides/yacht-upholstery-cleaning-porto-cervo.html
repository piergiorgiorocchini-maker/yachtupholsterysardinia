Why the sequence matters

Start with the surfaces the owner will see and use first.

Owner-arrival preparation is not the moment to clean every textile indiscriminately. The first task is to identify the visible, frequently used areas that influence the immediate impression of the interior.

Main salon seating, entrance-adjacent cushions, dining chairs and lounge areas normally deserve priority. Cabin textiles may follow when the scope, access and drying window allow it.

Send wide photographs and close-ups to Mr. Rocchini, together with the berth location and arrival deadline, for a realistic assessment before work is scheduled.

1. Inspect the yacht in natural and artificial light

Pale fabrics may look acceptable in soft cabin lighting and reveal traffic marks, drink residues, body-oil shading or uneven previous treatment once the blinds are opened. Review seating from the normal guest approach, not only from close range.

Photograph each complete seating group first, then capture individual marks. This preserves context and helps distinguish one localised problem from a larger cleaning scope.

2. Separate removable contamination from permanent change

Cleaning can address suspended soil, many fresh residues and some odour held in textile fibres. It cannot reliably reverse colour loss, abrasion, fibre distortion, sun fading or chemical damage caused by previous attempts.

A responsible assessment should identify these limits before the service, particularly on pale or delicate upholstery where unrealistic promises become very visible.

3. Prioritise high-visibility seating and cushions

Main salon sofas, lounge seating, dining chairs and cushions near entrances usually receive the closest attention. Decorative cushions should be identified separately from fixed seating because filling, trims and construction may require different moisture limits.

When time is restricted, a defined priority list is more useful than a vague request to “clean the interior.” It allows the crew and technician to agree on the areas that must be completed first.

4. Coordinate with housekeeping and technical work

Textile cleaning should follow dusty maintenance, sanding or contractor traffic whenever possible. Housekeeping can then complete surrounding hard surfaces after the textile process without recontaminating freshly prepared areas.

Access routes, shore power, water availability, marina clearance and onboard contact details should be confirmed before equipment reaches the berth. Porto Cervo schedules are rarely improved by discovering security procedures at the gate.

5. Protect a realistic drying window

Ventilation, textile construction, moisture level and cabin conditions determine return-to-use time. Cushions should not be restacked or placed tightly against surfaces before they are ready.

The owner-arrival deadline must therefore be included in the first message, not revealed after the work begins as a small theatrical surprise.

FAQ

Owner-arrival upholstery preparation: common questions

How early should upholstery be assessed before owner arrival?

As early as practical. The condition, scope, marina access and drying window should be understood before the final housekeeping sequence is fixed.

Can the first assessment be made from photographs?

Yes. Send wide images, close-ups, berth location, required areas and the exact arrival deadline by WhatsApp.

Should every cushion be cleaned?

Not automatically. Fixed seating, loose cushions and decorative pieces should be assessed according to condition, construction and visibility.
