Material and condition assessment

The visible mark is only part of the diagnosis.

Fibres, dyes, backing, pile condition, humidity, previous chemicals and permanent wear can change both the correct method and the realistic result.

Photographs and on-board inspection help distinguish removable contamination from fading, abrasion, dye loss, pile distortion or structural damage.

Pale and natural-fibre carpets Traffic lanes and compressed pile Food, drink and cosmetic marks Localised upholstery staining Textile-held odours Humidity-related concerns Previous treatment residues Restricted drying windows
Realistic limits matter. Permanent fading, abrasion, colour loss and physical damage cannot be cleaned away. These limitations are explained before work begins rather than after expectations have escaped into open water.

Operational questions

Useful answers before requesting the service.

What photographs and information should I send?

Send wide views of each area, close-ups of visible marks, the yacht or marina location, the number of areas to be treated and the required timing. Material details or manufacturer information are useful when available.

Can you work while the yacht is in berth?

Yes, subject to berth access, equipment movement, power availability, crew activity, ventilation and the agreed drying window. These points are confirmed before scheduling.

Can carpets, upholstery and mattresses be treated in one visit?

Often yes. A combined scope is practical when access, ventilation, service sequence and return-to-use requirements allow it.

How long will treated areas take to dry?

Drying depends on the fibre, construction, depth of treatment, air movement, humidity and ventilation. A realistic indication is given after the material and scope have been assessed.

Can every stain or visible mark be removed?

No. Results depend on contamination, fibre, dye stability, age, previous treatments and permanent wear or damage. The objective is the best safe and realistic result, not a promise invented before seeing the material.

Which Northern Sardinian locations do you cover?

Priority areas include Porto Cervo, Costa Smeralda, Portisco, Olbia, Porto Rotondo, Poltu Quatu, Cannigione, Golfo Aranci and Palau. Other locations are assessed according to timing and access.
